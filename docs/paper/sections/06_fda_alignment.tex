\section{Toward the Evidence the FDA Expects from EHR-Based CDS}
\label{sec:fda}

Recent United States Food and Drug Administration guidance on clinical
decision support software \citep{fda2022cds}, together with the
International Medical Device Regulators Forum's framework for the
clinical evaluation of software as a medical device
\citep{imdrf2017samd}, asks developers of EHR-based CDS to make the
basis of their recommendations transparent to the clinicians who use
them and to demonstrate validation in the populations of intended
use. Achieving that posture has historically been bottlenecked by the
same access constraints described in the introduction: the relevant
evidence is generated, if at all, one bilateral data use agreement at
a time \citep{finlayson2021clinicianshift,koch2024distributionshift,soin2025shiftremediation}.

FLAIR's contribution to that posture is structural rather than
declarative. Byte-identical task code stamped into every site's output
makes the basis of a model's behavior re-inspectable rather than
asserted; a reviewer can confirm that the cohort definition,
exclusions, and outcome label that produced a site's metrics are the
same ones that produced every other site's metrics, without taking
that on trust. The per-site discrimination, calibration, and fairness
JSONs surface the kind of population-level evidence that
regulator-facing dossiers and clinician-facing documentation
increasingly require, in a form that is mechanically auditable. And
running across 12+ heterogeneous academic medical centers without data
movement provides evidence of behavior under realistic distribution
shift, where the literature has repeatedly shown that performance can
silently degrade
\citep{wong2021external,finlayson2021clinicianshift,koch2024distributionshift}.

FLAIR is not a regulatory submission, not a substitute for prospective
clinical trials, and not a guarantee of clinical utility. It is the
layer of evidence between local model development and the
regulator- or clinician-facing claims a developer eventually wants to
make. That layer is currently missing for most published EHR CDS;
making it routine is the goal.
