Extubation Failure within 24 Hours

At the moment of the first confirmed extubation, predict whether the patient will require reintubation in the next 24 hours. Death without reintubation in that window is excluded to avoid labeling palliative extubations as failures. One prediction per hospitalization, anchored at a single per-encounter event. The key algorithmic ingredient is the consensus-window IMV episode detector (_ventilation_episodes.detect_imv_episodes), which distinguishes a true extubation from sensor flicker, tracheostomy sentinels, and intermittent ventilator swaps. Ambiguous censoring is dropped to avoid positive-unlabeled bias [Thille 2011].

The decision this supports is two-sided: extubate, or keep the patient intubated. It therefore acts on low risk (clinical_direction: "below") with a shipped policy of 0.10 — pull the tube when P(failure) < 10%, otherwise leave it in. Nothing else is actionable at this timestamp; a tracheostomy in particular is a decision made in a still-intubated patient around ventilator day 7–14, and lives in a different task.

Code note Implemented in extubation_failure_24h.py on the stitched-encounter grain: respiratory-support rows are relabeled to the stitched encounter before episode detection, so an intubation/extubation spanning a re-admission boundary is one episode. Constants verified against code: HORIZON_HOURS=24, INTUB_WINDOW_MIN_DEFAULT=15, EXTUB_WINDOW_MIN_DEFAULT=30, TRACH_CLOSURE_TOLERANCE_MIN=1, MIN_IMV_HOURS=6.

Consensus-window IMV episode detection

A CLIF respiratory_support row is a device-status sample, not a clinical event. A single misclassified row (brief sensor disconnect, vent swap, transient downgrade) should not be counted as intubation or extubation. detect_imv_episodes confirms each transition with a forward consensus window:

IMV start
A device imv row at time \(t\) opens an episode iff no contradicting non-IMV row falls in \((t,\, t + \texttt{INTUB\_WINDOW\_MIN}]\), with INTUB_WINDOW_MIN = 15 minutes.
Episode close (true extubation)
A non-IMV row at time \(t\) (while an episode is open) closes that episode iff no contradicting IMV row falls in \((t,\, t + \texttt{EXTUB\_WINDOW\_MIN}]\), with EXTUB_WINDOW_MIN = 30 minutes.
Trach sentinel
A row with device_category = "trach collar" or tracheostomy = true closes any open episode at its timestamp and halts further counting for the hospitalization. If the trach sentinel fires before any IMV row, is_pre_existing_trach = true.

The detector emits one row per confirmed episode: (hospitalization_id, episode_index, episode_start_dttm, episode_end_dttm, is_pre_existing_trach). The episode_end_dttm is null iff the patient is still on IMV at end of record or the episode was closed by a trach sentinel that did not first pass through a non-IMV transition.

FLAIR operational definition

Index time and label

Let \(H_i\) be a hospitalization that survives the cohort filters. Let \(E^1_i = (s^1_i, e^1_i)\) be its first IMV episode and \(E^2_i = (s^2_i, e^2_i)\) its second (if any).

Prediction time (t_extub)
\(t_{\text{extub}}^i = e^1_i = \) episode_end_dttm of the first episode. Exactly one decision point per hospitalization (one-per-stay).
Reintubation time
\(t_{\text{reint}}^i = s^2_i\) if \(E^2_i\) exists, \(+\infty\) otherwise.
Death time
\(t_{\text{death}}^i\) from hospitalization.death_dttm; \(+\infty\) if null.
Label horizon
\((t_{\text{extub}}^i,\, t_{\text{extub}}^i + 24\text{h}]\).
Label (label_extubation_failure_24h)
\[ y_i = \mathbb{1}\!\left[ t_{\text{reint}}^i \in (t_{\text{extub}}^i,\, t_{\text{extub}}^i + 24\text{h}] \right]. \]
Observability end
\(t_{\text{obs\_end}}^i = \texttt{coalesce}(t_{\text{death}}^i,\, t_{\text{disch}}^i)\).
Cohort retention
Keep the row iff \[ t_{\text{reint}}^i \in (t_{\text{extub}}^i, t_{\text{extub}}^i + 24\text{h}] \ \vee\ \left(t_{\text{death}}^i \notin (t_{\text{extub}}^i, t_{\text{extub}}^i + 24\text{h}] \ \wedge\ t_{\text{obs\_end}}^i \ge t_{\text{extub}}^i + 24\text{h}\right). \] A patient discharged alive less than 24h after extubation with no observed death or reintubation has unobserved outcome; treating it as \(y_i=0\) would inject positive-unlabeled bias. A patient who dies within 24h without reintubation is also dropped to exclude likely palliative extubations from the reintubation endpoint.

Cohort

  • Age at admission \(\ge 18\) with valid admission_dttm and discharge_dttm.
  • \(\ge 1\) ICU ADT record.
  • First IMV episode (episode_index = 1) has non-null episode_end_dttm (a confirmed extubation exists).
  • First IMV episode lasts \(\ge\) MIN_IMV_HOURS = 6 hours (episode_end_dttm − episode_start_dttm): a brief peri-procedural intubation is not the weaning decision this task models. The 15/30-minute consensus windows below are artifact filters, not a cohort criterion.
  • Exclude pre-existing tracheostomy (is_pre_existing_trach).
  • Exclude first episode closed by a trach sentinel (±1 min tolerance via TRACH_CLOSURE_TOLERANCE_MIN = 1; _drop_trach_closed).
  • Exclude death within 24h post-extubation without reintubation (palliative extubation).
  • Exclude ambiguous censoring (discharged alive <24h post-extubation with no death and no reintubation).

CLIF tables and categories used

Task T3 reads four CLIF v2.1 tables. The cohort entry filter is on adt.location_category; the index event is derived from two category-column predicates in respiratory_support.

CLIF tableCategory columnLiteral values kept (case-insensitive)
hospitalization—cohort/demographics join; no category filter
patient—demographics join; no category filter
adtlocation_category{"icu"}
respiratory_supportdevice_category, tracheostomy {"imv"} classifies a row as IMV; any other value is non-IMV. Trach sentinel: device_category = "trach collar" ∨ tracheostomy = true.

tracheostomy is a boolean, not a category. The trach sentinel fires when either the categorical device_category equals "trach collar" OR the boolean tracheostomy is true. A site that omits one signal can still detect tracheostomies via the other; sites omitting both will silently include trach-decannulation events in the extubation cohort.

Columns selected:

  • hospitalization: hospitalization_id, patient_id, admission_dttm, discharge_dttm, age_at_admission, discharge_category (loaded but not filtered).
  • patient: patient_id, sex_category, race_category, ethnicity_category, death_dttm.
  • adt: hospitalization_id, location_category, in_dttm, out_dttm.
  • respiratory_support: hospitalization_id, recorded_dttm, device_category, tracheostomy.

Build pipeline for T3. Solid arrows: main flow. Dashed red arrows: exclusions. The diamond enforces the endpoint rule: reintubations within 24h are positive; death without reintubation in that window is dropped; and any hospitalization whose observation window closes before \(t_{\text{extub}}+24\text{h}\) without reintubation is dropped because its negative label is unobservable.

  1. Load CLIF tables: hospitalization, patient, adt, respiratory_support.
  2. Apply adult-ICU cohort filters.
  3. load_respiratory_support(cfg, hosp_ids) returns all device-status rows for the cohort.
  4. Run detect_imv_episodes(resp) (consensus detector above).
  5. Filter to first episodes ending in a confirmed extubation: episode_index = 1 ∧ episode_end_dttm ≠ ∅ ∧ ¬is_pre_existing_trach.
  6. Drop first episodes shorter than MIN_IMV_HOURS = 6: episode_end_dttm − episode_start_dttm ≥ 6h, equivalently \(t_{\text{extub}}^i - t_{\text{intub}}^i \ge 6\text{h}\).
  7. Drop trach-closed first episodes via _drop_trach_closed: any first episode whose episode_end_dttm coincides with a trach collar or tracheostomy=true row within ±1 minute (decannulation ≠ extubation).
  8. Extract second-episode starts as \(t_{\text{reint}}^i\) = episode_start_dttm where episode_index = 2 (left join; null if none).
  9. Join encounter-level \(t_{\text{death}}^i\) and \(t_{\text{disch}}^i\).
  10. Compute \(y_i = \texttt{\_reintub}\).
  11. Drop death-only palliative extubations and ambiguous censoring rows (the diamond).
  12. Set window_start = t_extub − 24h, window_end = t_extub (fixed 24h lookback).
  13. Apply temporal split, keyed on \(t_{\text{extub}}^i\); select OUTPUT_COLS; persist as parquet.

Design choices and rationale

Why 15-minute / 30-minute consensus windows

RespiratorySupport in CLIF is a device-status sample: brief sensor dropouts, vent swaps, and transient downgrades produce single rows that look like transitions but are not. The 15-minute IMV consensus on the start side and the 30-minute non-IMV consensus on the close side filter these artifacts while still resolving real extubations within a clinically reasonable margin. The asymmetry (15 in, 30 out) reflects asymmetric cost: a missed intubation start loses only a small slice of input-feature window, whereas a false extubation close anchors the entire label.

Why drop trach-closed first episodes

The state machine treats a trach sentinel as an episode close so counting halts cleanly, but a tracheostomy decannulation is not the clinical event this task predicts. Modeling it as an extubation would mix a planned airway-management procedure with the unplanned reintubation-vs-failure decision, inflating the cohort with patients whose 24h outcome is dominated by trach-specific physiology.

Why drop ambiguous censoring

A patient extubated at 03:00 and discharged alive at 18:00 the same day with no death and no reintubation has only 15 hours of post-extubation observability. Labeling them \(y_i=0\) treats absence of observed failure as evidence of success when it is just censoring — a textbook positive-unlabeled trap. The cohort filter resolves it by dropping the unobservable row. The cost is a small positive-skew in the retained cohort (patients with longer observation are slightly sicker), acknowledged in the paper's Discussion.

Why one-per-stay (not continuous)

The decision to extubate is itself the clinical handle. Predictions at arbitrary timesteps before the actual extubation are not actionable because they would be evaluated against a different intervention (extubation at a different time). Restricting to the actual extubation moment makes the label well-defined and the comparison across hospitalizations meaningful.

Prevalence implications

MIMIC-CLIF: 26,771 extubation episodes pass the cohort filters, 1,456 positive (5.4%). Reporting AUPRC alongside AUROC is mandatory.

Why the threshold is 0.10

A decision threshold is not a cutoff, it is the exchange rate between the two harms. You are indifferent between the arms when t / (1−t) = harm(one unnecessary continued intubation) / harm(one failed extubation), so 0.10 encodes 1:9 — a failed extubation is nine times worse than keeping a ready patient intubated. Epstein & Ciubotaru report 43% mortality in reintubated patients against 12% in successfully extubated; discounted heavily for confounding by indication, 9× is defensible where 1:4 (t = 0.20) is not.

Mind the horizon. The familiar “acceptable reintubation rate is 10–15%” figure is a 48–72h number; this label is 24h and roughly half of reintubations fall outside it, which is why the observed rate above is 5.4% rather than ~12%. A threshold anchored to 72h intuition is inflated for a 24h label. At 0.10 the policy sits at ~1.85× the base rate: discriminating, but reaching a real fraction of patients.

Two quantities land in this numeric range and must not be conflated. The population reintubation rate is what happens across all extubations; the threshold is the individual risk at which the action changes. They connect only through revealed preference — a unit running at 10% and calling that acceptable is saying its marginal extubated patient carries ~10% risk.

Limitation — the unobserved counterfactual

100% of this cohort was extubated. “Do not extubate” is therefore a recommendation the data cannot validate: there are no held-back patients to observe. On the decision curve this makes treat-all equal to observed current practice, which is the right comparator and a demanding one — at a 5.4% failure rate, extubating everyone is correct 94.6% of the time. But any net benefit above that line assumes the failures of the patients the model would have held back would have been avoided by waiting, and this dataset cannot test that assumption. Read the decision curve as “does selective extubation beat extubating everyone, under that assumption”, never as an estimate of realized benefit.

For the same reason the feature window is cut strictly before prediction_dttm (feature_cutoff_dttm): that timestamp is the first non-IMV respiratory-support row, i.e. the row announcing the extubation. A model asked “should we extubate?” must not be shown the extubation.

Out of scope

  • Validation of a withhold-extubation policy — see the limitation above.
  • Second-, third-, and later-extubation decision points.
  • Death without reintubation as a failure mode (dropped to exclude palliative extubations).
  • Non-invasive ventilation (NIV) as a failure mode (currently only reintubation counts).
  • A continuous variant predicting extubation failure ahead of the actual extubation.
  • Curated drug-list or ventilator-mode feature design.
