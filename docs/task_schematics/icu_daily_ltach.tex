\begin{abstract}
\noindent We specify the FLAIR daily ICU LTACH-discharge task: at
every local 07:00 rounds anchor during the first ICU stay, predict
whether the hospitalization will end with transfer to a Long-Term
Acute Care Hospital (LTACH) rather than in-hospital mortality
(Expired or Hospice). Task~T2 is the exact label complement of
Task~T1 on the identical cohort, identical grid, and identical
24-hour lookback. This schematic specifies only the differences;
the shared pipeline is the authority defined in
\texttt{icu\_daily\_mortality.tex} (Task~T1).
\end{abstract}

\section{Relationship to Task~T1}

The build pipeline of Task~T2 is byte-identical to Task~T1's: same
cohort filters (adult, $\geq 1$ ICU ADT, \texttt{discharge\_category}
$\in \{\text{LTACH},\,\text{Expired},\,\text{Hospice}\}$), same
$\predstart$, $\predstop$, and decision-point grid, same 24h
lookback, same temporal split. The only difference is the final label
rule. The full operational definition, cohort details, and step-by-step
build logic are in \texttt{icu\_daily\_mortality.tex} (Task~T1); the
flowchart of Figure~\ref{fig:t2-flow} is shared.

\section{Label rule (Task~T2 only)}

Let
$\texttt{disp}_i = \texttt{lower}(\texttt{strip}(\texttt{discharge\_category}_i))$.
For $d$ in the daily 07:00 decision grid $D_i$:
\[
y_i(d) \;=\; \mathbb{1}\!\left[\,\texttt{disp}_i\ \text{matches regex}\
\texttt{ltach|long.?term}\,\right].
\]

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item The label is constant across all rows of $H_i$ (the eventual
        disposition is a single hospitalization-level fact broadcast
        to every grid point).
  \item Because the Task~T1 / Task~T2 cohort filter guarantees
        $\texttt{disp}_i$ is either \texttt{"expired"},
        \texttt{"hospice"}, or matches the LTACH regex,
        $\texttt{label\_ltach}_i + \texttt{label\_mortality}_i = 1$
        for every retained row.
\end{itemize}

\section{CLIF tables and categories used}

The CLIF surface for Task~T2 is identical to Task~T1: the same three
tables (\texttt{hospitalization}, \texttt{patient}, \texttt{adt})
and the same category-column filters. The cohort filter on
\texttt{hospitalization.discharge\_category} keeps
$\{\texttt{"expired"},\,\texttt{"hospice"}\}\ \cup\ $regex
\texttt{ltach|long.?term} (case-insensitive); the ADT filter restricts
to \texttt{location\_category} $=$ \texttt{"icu"} (case-insensitive);
\texttt{patient} is join-only. See the full table and per-table column
inventory in the sibling document \texttt{icu\_daily\_mortality.tex}
(\S CLIF tables and categories used). Tasks~T1 and~T2 differ only in
the final label predicate, not in which CLIF data they touch.

\section{Design choices and rationale}

\paragraph{Why a separate schematic for the complement label.}
A reader scanning the supplement should find one document per FLAIR
task, with its own META, label rule, and prevalence numbers. The
build pipeline being shared is a convenience of implementation, not a
property of the task definition: a future site whose
\texttt{discharge\_category} taxonomy splits LTACH from other
long-term destinations more finely could change Task~T2's regex
without affecting Task~T1.

\paragraph{Prevalence asymmetry vs.\ Task~T1.}
At the row level the positive rate is 42.6\% LTACH, complementary to
T1's 57.4\% mortality. At the stay level the asymmetry is sharper:
32.9\% of retained stays are LTACH-positive vs.\ 67.1\% mortality-positive
(see \texttt{docs/paper/outline.tex} Table~1). In MIMIC, hospice and
expired-in-hospital both fall on T1's positive side, while only the
LTACH-or-long-term family falls on T2's, so the marginals are not
50/50. Reporting AUPRC alongside AUROC is mandatory.

\paragraph{Why LTACH is the operationally interesting label.}
LTACH transfer is a planned, resource-intensive disposition decision
that triggers care-coordination work several days before discharge
\citep{kahn2010ltach}. A morning prediction of LTACH-bound vs.\
in-hospital-mortality trajectory can drive social-work referrals,
family conversations, and family-meeting timing; the same trajectory
forecast from the mortality side (T1) is more commonly used for
goals-of-care conversations. The two complementary framings expose
the same underlying signal to two distinct downstream workflows.
