AKI KDIGO-2 within 24h (continuous)

Every hour, predict whether the patient will meet KDIGO stage-2 AKI criteria in the next 24 hours. Continuous risk stream — stops the moment KDIGO-2 fires, so the label never leaks.

deterioration continuous one-per-timestep binary

Reference: Koyner JL, et al. Crit Care Med, 2018 — doi:10.1097/CCM.0000000000003123.

Note on the input window. The window above is the minimum guaranteed lookback, not a cap. The model is free to use any data observed before the prediction time — including data from previous hospitalizations of the same patient. Only data at or after the prediction time would constitute leakage.

Clinical motivation

AKI is one of the most common ICU complications and one of the most preventable when caught early — adjust nephrotoxic dosing, hold ACEi, target MAP, watch fluid balance. KDIGO-2 specifically signals the threshold where intervention changes management. A continuous 24h-ahead risk stream lets the EHR fire timely alerts rather than a one-shot "AKI risk at admission."

Why it's hard

  • Hourly rows × 24h lookahead = sequential prediction problem; auto-correlation is huge.
  • Label-leakage is structural — we have to stop emitting at first KDIGO-2 onset to keep labels valid.
  • SCr measurement frequency is non-uniform (q6h vs q24h vs spot) — sites differ, and KDIGO-2 detection depends on it.
  • Class imbalance per row is severe; per-patient stays_ever_positive is the more honest operating metric.
