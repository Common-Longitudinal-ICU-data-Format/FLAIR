Extubation failure within 24h

At the moment of first confirmed extubation, will the patient accumulate less than 1 ventilator-free day in the next 24 hours — i.e., die or get reintubated? One prediction per ICU stay, anchored on the extubation event.

intervention episode one-per-stay binary

Note on the input window. The window above is the minimum guaranteed lookback, not a cap. The model is free to use any data observed before the prediction time — including the full IMV episode and any prior hospitalizations. Only data at or after the prediction time would constitute leakage.

Clinical motivation

Premature extubation drives reintubation, prolonged ICU stay, and excess mortality; over-cautious extubation prolongs ventilation, sedation needs, and ICU-acquired complications. A reliable 24h failure-risk estimate at the moment the team is weighing extubation is one of the highest-yield CDSS targets in critical care. The consensus state-machine for IMV episodes filters the noisy transitions that confound naive end-of-IMV labels.

Why it's hard

  • Single anchor per stay → small positive class, hard to learn temporal trends.
  • Selection bias: only patients the team thought ready get extubated, so the cohort is conditioned on a clinician judgment we can't see.
  • Trach trajectory and ambiguous early discharges need explicit censoring rules.
  • Site-to-site ventilator weaning protocols differ — reintubation rates carry institutional signal.
