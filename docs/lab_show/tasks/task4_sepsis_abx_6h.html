Task 4 of 5

Sepsis (antibiotic-anchored) within 6h

Hourly across the hospital stay, predict whether the patient will receive their first qualifying systemic-IV antibiotic in the next 6 hours. The antibiotic order is used as an operational proxy for clinician recognition of sepsis. Prediction grid stops strictly before t_zero to prevent label leakage.

intervention continuous one-per-timestep binary

Reference: Kamran F, Tjandra D, Heiler A, Virzi J, Singh K, King JE, Valley TS, Wiens J. NEJM AI 1(3), 2024 — doi:10.1056/AIoa2300032.

Clinical motivation

Antibiotic-anchored sepsis labels sidestep the brittleness of consensus-derived sepsis definitions (Sepsis-3, CMS SEP-1) and are reproducible across sites with only medication and ADT data. A 6-hour forecast horizon aligns with bedside workflow: it's short enough that an alert prompts action, and long enough that the model has room to detect deterioration before the team commits to a sepsis bundle.

Why it's hard

  • Label-leakage is structural — grid must stop strictly before t_zero or the model trivially memorizes the action.
  • Hospital-wide cohort means most rows are negative; per-row positive rate is small even though stays-ever-positive can be ~5–10%.
  • Antibiotic stewardship practices vary across sites → the label captures local prescribing culture as well as biology.
  • Vital-sign sparsity dictates pred_start; sites with delayed vitals capture get shorter risk windows.
