Clinical motivation

LTACH placement is a major resource decision — bed availability, payer negotiation, family coordination — and clinicians often realise too late that a patient is headed there. Pairing this task with Task 1 lets a single model output both tails of the terminal-trajectory question and lets calibration be checked symmetrically across the two outcomes.

Why it's hard

  • Pair complement of Task 1 — any modeling bias in one is reflected in the other.
  • LTACH placement signals (long IMV runs, slow weaning, persistent vasopressor need) overlap heavily with mortality signals.
  • Site-to-site LTACH availability varies → label distribution is partly an operational artifact, not purely clinical.
  • Cohort is conditioned on a discharge-time outcome that's invisible at prediction time.
